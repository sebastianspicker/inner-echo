# Experience interpretation and evidence boundaries

Reviewed 9 September 2026. This note connects the bundled collections to descriptions of reported
experiences and explains the limits of the audiovisual design. It is not a clinical validation study.

## What the evidence can support

A source may support the existence of a reported experience, its variability, or an association seen
in a study. It does not establish a matching image distortion, sound, rhythm, or filter. No source
reviewed here validates Inner Echo's effects, weights, numerical settings, or audiovisual coupling as
a reproduction of a condition.

The product therefore labels experience evidence separately from artistic mappings. Profile weights
express authoring emphasis, not prevalence, severity, or diagnostic probability. Intensity controls
the effects only. Audio and camera metrics drive optional artistic responses; they do not measure
mental state.

## Collections and design decisions

| Collection | Supported scope and limitation | Audiovisual interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Anxiety-related tension and worry | NIMH describes excessive worry, tension, and concentration difficulties in GAD, with symptoms that fluctuate. This does not establish tunnel vision, or combine GAD and social anxiety into one experience. [GAD source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/generalized-anxiety-disorder-gad) | Mild focus narrowing and detail, quiet texture, and shallow sound modulation. These are adjustable metaphors for tension, not perceptual symptoms. |
| Panic-related alarm | NIMH describes episodes of intense fear with possible physical symptoms. It does not establish a particular visual waveform or heartbeat sound. [Panic source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms) | A shallow, slow visual wave with an optional steady sound bed. The collection includes no heartbeat-like tone and no sound-driven alarm escalation; the wave rate is a design choice. |
| PTSD-related experiences | NIMH describes re-experiencing, avoidance, arousal/reactivity, and cognition/mood symptoms. Reminders can prompt symptoms; exposure to trauma alone is not PTSD. [PTSD source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd) | Restrained detail and static texture with quiet sound. No automatic intrusion bursts, memory replay, or graphic imagery. This limited collection does not represent all symptom clusters. |
| OCD-related intrusive thoughts and repetition | NIMH describes unwanted intrusive thoughts and mental or physical compulsions with varied content. Ordering is one possible presentation, not the definition of OCD, and ordinary repetition is not equivalent to OCD. [OCD source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-when-unwanted-thoughts-or-repetitive-behaviors-take-over) | Subtle image persistence and quiet echo, adjustable down to no visual persistence. No default grid, ordering task, or insistent audio rhythm. Rumination is not treated as interchangeable with obsession. |
| ADHD-related attention experiences | NIMH describes varied patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity, including differences with age. This does not imply chaotic vision or universal sensory overload. [ADHD source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know) | Gentle changes in areas of emphasis without whole-image jitter, with a steady optional sound bed. Sensory overload stays a separate selectable dimension. |
| Depression-related energy and concentration | NIMH describes possible loss of interest, reduced energy, and concentration difficulty; symptoms differ between people. This does not mean people see a grey or blurred world. [Depression source](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression) | Mild tonal softening, adjustable blur, and quiet filtered sound. No default temporal smear or pitch wobble. Colour treatment is an artistic choice, not a universal perception. |
| Depersonalization / derealization experiences | The research notes describe detachment and experiences of unreality. Experimental findings do not establish chromatic splitting, delayed self-images, or a universal audiovisual signature. [Existing evidence notes](remaining-dimensions.md) | A light veil and filtered sound without default feedback, refraction, chromatic splitting, pitch wobble, or delay. This does not reproduce bodily detachment or altered reality. |

These are design judgments informed by the evidence boundaries, not research findings that the chosen
effects are beneficial or comfortable for every person. Smooth transitions and restrained values are
engineering choices. The sound beds are not treatments, entrainment protocols, or condition-specific
frequencies.

## Research qualifications

Merritt Millman, L. S., Huang, X., Wainipitapong, S., Medford, N., and Pick, S. (2024). *Behavioural,
autonomic, and neural responsivity in depersonalisation-derealisation disorder: A systematic review of
experimental evidence*. Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 163, 105783.
[DOI and publication record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105783). This review concerns
experimental responsivity, with heterogeneous findings and methodological limits. It does not establish
a visual or acoustic signature for a camera overlay.

Micoulaud-Franchi, J.-A., et al. (2015). *Sensory gating in adult with attention-deficit/hyperactivity
disorder: Event-evoked potential and perceptual experience reports comparisons with schizophrenia*.
Biological Psychology, 107, 16–23. [Publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25766264/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2015.03.002). This small comparative adult study used an
auditory electrophysiological measure and self-report. Its group findings do not establish universal
sensory overload in ADHD, and do not validate visual jitter, moving markers, or sound modulation. That
is why sensory overload stays separately selectable rather than defining the ADHD collection.

## Choice and implementation

Camera, sound, and microphone activation stay separate actions. Fresh sessions start with audiovisual
coupling at zero; you can choose a response strength in Advanced composition. Saved settings remain
compatible and may contain an earlier response strength. Selecting a profile alone does not activate
media. Reduced Motion removes motion effects; Safe Mode applies the existing clamps; Stop Everything
releases active resources.

The bundled condition pages list the actual video and enabled audio stacks. Dimension compositions have
their own motif lists and can differ from a curated collection. Legacy effects remain available to
compatible saved profiles and runtime contracts, but their existence is not evidence for assigning them
to a condition.

## Limits of this review

The sources above describe conditions and experiences. They do not validate a simulation, an empathy
outcome, accessibility for every viewer, or clinical usefulness. No lived-experience participant
review, clinical evaluation, or controlled comfort study was performed for these changes. Those forms
of evaluation would be needed before claiming representational adequacy or measured benefits.
